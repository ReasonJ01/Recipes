---
title: "Lemon Drizzle Loaf with Crunchy Sugar Crust & Caramelised Lemon"
---

## Ingredients
- Cake: 225 g unsalted butter, softened
- Cake: 225 g caster sugar
- Cake: 4 large eggs, room temperature
- Cake: 225 g self-raising flour
- Cake: Finely grated zest of 1 unwaxed lemon
- Cake: 1 tsp vanilla extract
- Tin: About 15 g soft butter
- Tin: 35–40 g demerara sugar
- Crunchy lemon drizzle: 40 g fresh lemon juice
- Crunchy lemon drizzle: 100 g granulated sugar
- Candied caramelised lemons: 1 small unwaxed lemon
- Candied caramelised lemons: 100 g caster sugar
- Candied caramelised lemons: 100 ml water
- Candied caramelised lemons: 10–15 g caster or granulated sugar, for caramelising

## Instructions
1. Start the lemon slices first. Slice the small lemon into rounds about 3 mm thick, remove the seeds, and select 4–6 good central slices.
2. Put 100 g caster sugar and 100 ml water into a wide pan and bring to the boil. Add the lemon slices, reduce to a steady gentle simmer, and cook for 10–15 minutes, until the flesh and white pith look translucent; judge by translucency rather than the clock.
3. Lift each slice out with a fork or small palette knife, let excess syrup drip back into the saucepan, then transfer the slices to a wire rack to drain and dry while you make and bake the cake.
4. Heat the oven to 180°C conventional / 160°C fan.
5. Use the loaf tin without parchment. Butter the entire inside generously and evenly, including the corners, with about 15 g soft butter. Add 35–40 g demerara sugar, tilt and rotate the tin until the base and all four sides are thoroughly coated, and tip out anything that genuinely will not stick.
6. For a more reliable sugar coating, chill the prepared tin for 10 minutes while you make the batter so the butter sets and the batter is less likely to sweep the sugar away.
7. Beat 225 g softened butter and 225 g caster sugar for 3–5 minutes, until considerably paler and fluffy.
8. Beat in the 4 eggs one at a time, incorporating each reasonably well before adding the next.
9. Add the finely grated zest of 1 lemon and 1 tsp vanilla extract and mix briefly.
10. Fold in 225 g self-raising flour and stop as soon as no dry flour remains; do not continue beating.
11. Take the chilled tin from the fridge. Spoon the batter into the centre rather than scraping it heavily against the sugared walls, gently level it, and fill the tin only 2/3 full regardless of its nominal size. Put any leftover batter into muffin cases or another small ovenproof dish.
12. Bake on the middle shelf. For a typical larger loaf, expect roughly 45–50 minutes; for a smaller tin, begin checking around 30–35 minutes.
13. The cake is done when it is well risen and cracked, a skewer inserted into the centre comes out clean or with a few moist crumbs, and the middle springs back gently. Do not extend the bake simply to darken the sugared exterior, as this will dry out the crumb.
14. Make the crunchy drizzle only when the cake is almost finished baking. Very briefly mix 40 g fresh lemon juice with 100 g granulated sugar; it should remain a thick, gritty slurry with lots of clearly visible sugar crystals. Do not heat it, leave it sitting around, or stir it until smooth.
15. When the cake comes out of the oven, leave it in the tin for about 5 minutes, then prick the top deeply with a thin skewer, especially around the central crack.
16. Give the drizzle another very brief stir and spoon it over the hot cake in about three passes rather than pouring it all on at once. First spoon over some of the more liquid part so lemon juice penetrates the holes, wait about 30 seconds, then spoon over more of the gritty mixture, wait another 30 seconds, and finally deliberately distribute the remaining wet sugar crystals over the surface.
17. Finish with visible sugar sitting on top and do not smooth it. Keep as much drizzle as possible off the sides so the demerara exterior remains crisp.
18. Leave the cake for about 8–10 minutes after drizzling. Run a thin knife carefully around the perimeter, put a cooling rack or board over the tin, invert confidently and lift the tin away, then turn the cake upright again. If it resists, warm the outside and base of the tin briefly with a hot damp tea towel before trying again.
19. Cool the loaf completely on a rack.
20. Check the candied lemon slices. They should be translucent, flexible, no longer dripping, and tacky rather than soaking wet. If they are still very wet, put the rack into a 90–100°C oven for about 20–30 minutes, then cool them before caramelising.
21. Put the best 3–4 slices on a plate and sprinkle one side lightly with 10–15 g caster or granulated sugar.
22. Heat a good non-stick frying pan over medium to medium-high heat. Put the lemon slices in sugared-side down and leave them alone initially. After roughly 20–40 seconds, lift an edge carefully and inspect; look for deep amber patches, dark caramel, and perhaps a few almost-black areas, watching the colour rather than relying on the clock.
23. Do not caramelise the second side. Transfer the slices immediately to baking parchment or a silicone mat with the caramelised face upwards and leave until completely cool so the caramel firms.
24. Once both the loaf and lemon slices are completely cool, arrange 2–3 caramelised lemon slices along the central crack. A restrained arrangement of one full round, one overlapping full round, and one half or smaller slice works well.
25. If necessary, use a tiny dab of the leftover candying syrup underneath each slice to hold it in place, but do not brush syrup over the finished crunchy top.

## Notes
- You will want 2–3 lemons total.
- Use ordinary fresh lemon juice rather than charred lemon juice.
- Ordinary granulated sugar is preferable to caster sugar for the drizzle because the larger crystals are less eager to dissolve and produce a more obvious crunchy crust.
- The key change from the previous version is the drizzle ratio: 40 g lemon juice to 100 g granulated sugar, with the grittiest, sugar-rich portion added last. You should be able to see actual crystals covering much of the cake when you finish drizzling.
- The bare, buttered and demerara-sugared tin is intentional and should give the loaf a crisp exterior.
- Fill the loaf tin only 2/3 full regardless of its nominal size.
- Vanilla stays in the sponge.
